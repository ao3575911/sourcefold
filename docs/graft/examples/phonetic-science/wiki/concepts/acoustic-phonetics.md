# Acoustic phonetics

Acoustic phonetics studies the physical properties of speech sounds as
sound waves.

**Fundamental frequency (F0)** is the rate of vocal fold vibration,
heard as pitch. Typical speaking averages are roughly 85–180 Hz for
adult male voices and 165–255 Hz for adult female voices.

**Formants** are resonant frequencies of the vocal tract. The two
lowest, F1 and F2, largely determine the quality of a vowel (see
[vowels](vowels.md)): F1 varies inversely with vowel height (high
vowels have low F1), and F2 correlates with frontness (front vowels
have high F2). For /i/, F1 is low and F2 high; for /ɑ/, F1 is high and
F2 low.

A **spectrogram** plots time (x-axis) against frequency (y-axis) with
intensity as darkness. Vowels show dark formant bands; stops show a
silent gap followed by a release burst; fricatives show high-frequency
aperiodic noise (see [consonant
articulation](consonant-articulation.md) for the articulatory
classes).

**Voice onset time (VOT)** is the interval between a stop's release
and the start of voicing. English voiceless stops in stressed onsets
are aspirated with long-lag VOT (about 60–100 ms); voiced stops have
short-lag VOT near zero.

The **source–filter model** treats the larynx as the sound source and
the vocal tract as a filter that shapes the source spectrum into
recognizable speech sounds.
