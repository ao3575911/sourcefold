# Vowels

Vowels are produced with an open vocal tract — in contrast to the
constrictions of [consonant articulation](consonant-articulation.md) —
and are described by **tongue height** (high/close, mid, low/open),
**tongue backness** (front, central, back), and **lip rounding**
(rounded vs. unrounded).

Reference points: /i/ is a high front unrounded vowel (beet); /u/ is a
high back rounded vowel (boot); /ɑ/ is a low back unrounded vowel
(father); /æ/ is a low front unrounded vowel (cat). The mid central
unstressed vowel /ə/ (schwa) appears in the first syllable of "about".

The **vowel quadrilateral** maps vowels by height (vertical) and
backness (horizontal), following the IPA vowel chart.

**Monophthongs** hold one quality; **diphthongs** glide between two
qualities within a syllable, as in /aɪ/ (buy), /aʊ/ (cow), /ɔɪ/ (boy).

**Tense** vowels (/i, u/) are typically longer and can end a stressed
open syllable in English; **lax** vowels (/ɪ, ʊ, ɛ/) are shorter and
generally require a following consonant in stressed syllables.

Vowel quality has a direct acoustic correlate in formant frequencies —
see [acoustic phonetics](acoustic-phonetics.md).
