# Consonant articulation

Consonants are described by three parameters: **voicing**, **place of
articulation**, and **manner of articulation**. The International
Phonetic Alphabet (IPA) assigns one symbol per sound.

**Voicing:** the vocal folds vibrate for voiced sounds (/b, d, g, z/)
and do not for voiceless sounds (/p, t, k, s/).

**Place of articulation** — where airflow is constricted: bilabial
(/p, b, m/), labiodental (/f, v/), dental (/θ, ð/), alveolar
(/t, d, s, z, n, l/), postalveolar (/ʃ, ʒ/), palatal (/j/), velar
(/k, g, ŋ/), glottal (/h, ʔ/).

**Manner of articulation** — how airflow is constricted:

- stops/plosives fully block airflow (/p, t, k/);
- fricatives force air through a narrow channel (/f, s, ʃ/);
- affricates combine a stop with a fricative release (/tʃ, dʒ/);
- nasals lower the velum so air exits the nose (/m, n, ŋ/);
- approximants involve little constriction (/w, j, ɹ, l/).

Each consonant is a three-term label: /s/ is a voiceless alveolar
fricative; /m/ is a voiced bilabial nasal.

Sounds made with an open vocal tract are described separately — see
[vowels](vowels.md); their physical signatures appear under
[acoustic phonetics](acoustic-phonetics.md).
