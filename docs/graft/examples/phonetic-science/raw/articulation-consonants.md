# Notes: articulation of consonants

Consonants are described by three parameters: voicing, place of
articulation, and manner of articulation.

Voicing: vocal folds vibrate for voiced sounds (/b, d, g, z/), not for
voiceless sounds (/p, t, k, s/).

Place of articulation (where airflow is constricted): bilabial (/p, b,
m/), labiodental (/f, v/), dental (/θ, ð/), alveolar (/t, d, s, z, n,
l/), postalveolar (/ʃ, ʒ/), palatal (/j/), velar (/k, g, ŋ/), glottal
(/h, ʔ/).

Manner of articulation (how airflow is constricted): stops/plosives
fully block airflow (/p, t, k/); fricatives force air through a narrow
channel (/f, s, ʃ/); affricates combine a stop with a fricative
release (/tʃ, dʒ/); nasals lower the velum so air exits the nose
(/m, n, ŋ/); approximants involve little constriction (/w, j, ɹ, l/).

Each consonant is a three-term label, e.g. /s/ is a voiceless alveolar
fricative and /m/ is a voiced bilabial nasal. The International
Phonetic Alphabet (IPA) assigns one symbol per sound.
