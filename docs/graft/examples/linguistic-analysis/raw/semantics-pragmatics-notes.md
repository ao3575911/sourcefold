# Notes: semantics and pragmatics

Semantics studies literal meaning; pragmatics studies meaning in
context.

Lexical semantic relations: synonymy (couch/sofa), antonymy
(hot/cold), hyponymy (a dog is a kind of animal — dog is a hyponym of
animal), polysemy (one word, related senses: "head" of a body, of a
department).

Compositionality: the meaning of a sentence is built from the meanings
of its parts and their syntactic arrangement.

Truth conditions: to know a declarative sentence's meaning is to know
the conditions under which it would be true. Entailment: sentence A
entails B when B must be true whenever A is true ("Ana ate an apple"
entails "Ana ate something").

Pragmatics:

- Deixis: words like "here", "now", "I" depend on the speech context.
- Implicature: "Some students passed" typically implicates not all
  did, via Grice's cooperative principle and its maxims (quantity,
  quality, relation, manner).
- Speech acts: utterances perform actions — asserting, asking,
  promising ("I hereby promise…").
- Presupposition: "Pat stopped smoking" presupposes Pat used to smoke.
