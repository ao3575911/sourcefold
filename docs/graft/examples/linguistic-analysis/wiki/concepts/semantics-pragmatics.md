# Semantics and pragmatics

**Semantics** studies literal meaning; **pragmatics** studies meaning
in context.

## Lexical semantic relations

- Synonymy (couch/sofa) and antonymy (hot/cold).
- Hyponymy: a dog is a kind of animal — dog is a hyponym of animal.
- Polysemy: one word with related senses ("head" of a body, of a
  department).

## Sentence meaning

**Compositionality:** the meaning of a sentence is built from the
meanings of its parts and their syntactic arrangement (see
[syntax and constituency](syntax-constituency.md)).

**Truth conditions:** to know a declarative sentence's meaning is to
know the conditions under which it would be true. **Entailment:**
sentence A entails B when B must be true whenever A is true ("Ana ate
an apple" entails "Ana ate something").

## Pragmatics

- **Deixis:** words like "here", "now", "I" depend on the speech
  context.
- **Implicature:** "Some students passed" typically implicates not
  all did, via Grice's cooperative principle and its maxims
  (quantity, quality, relation, manner).
- **Speech acts:** utterances perform actions — asserting, asking,
  promising ("I hereby promise…").
- **Presupposition:** "Pat stopped smoking" presupposes Pat used to
  smoke.
