# Morphology

Morphology studies the internal structure of words. The **morpheme**
is the smallest meaningful unit of language. **Free morphemes** stand
alone as words (dog, run); **bound morphemes** must attach to another
form (un-, -ness, -s).

Affixes are classified by position: **prefixes** attach before a stem
and **suffixes** after it. English uses both; infixes and circumfixes
occur in other languages.

**Derivational morphology** creates new lexemes, often changing
category: happy → happiness (adjective → noun). **Inflectional
morphology** marks grammatical features without changing category:
walk → walked (tense), cat → cats (number). English has a small
inflectional inventory of eight regular inflectional suffixes.

Morphological analysis segments a word into morphemes and labels each:
un-help-ful-ness = negative prefix + root + adjectivizer +
nominalizer.

**Allomorphy** is one morpheme with several realizations: the English
plural appears as /s/ (cats), /z/ (dogs), and /əz/ (horses),
conditioned by the final sound of the stem.

Morphemes combine into phrases and sentences, the domain of
[syntax](syntax-constituency.md).
